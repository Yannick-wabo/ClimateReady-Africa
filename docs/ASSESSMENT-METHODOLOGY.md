# Assessment methodology

## Purpose

The rubric helps a project team identify whether essential evidence is missing, in progress or documented. It is an early-stage preparation aid and not an official Article 6 eligibility assessment.

## Pillars

| Pillar | Focus |
|---|---|
| Policy and eligibility | NDC alignment and host-country participation |
| Carbon integrity | Additionality, methodology, permanence and double-counting controls |
| MRV and verification | Monitoring, reporting, quality control and independent assurance |
| People and safeguards | Environmental and social risks, consultation, rights and benefits |
| Market pathway | Approval, authorisation, tracking, accounting and reporting |

## Ratings

- `Not yet` = 0
- `In progress` = 0.5
- `Documented` with at least ten characters of supporting reference or note = 1

The score is:

`100 × sum(applicable criterion weight × rating value) / sum(applicable criterion weights)`

The result is rounded to the nearest whole number. Criteria that do not apply to the selected sector, pathway or intended use are excluded from both numerator and denominator.

## Status rules

- `Not assessed`: no applicable criterion has a rating.
- `Early stage`: score below 50.
- `Needs work`: score at least 50 but the evidence-ready conditions are not met.
- `Evidence ready`: score at least 80, every applicable criterion is assessed and every applicable critical criterion is documented.

Weights, thresholds and status labels are application design decisions. They are not UNFCCC, PACM, national authority, crediting-programme or ICVCM ratings.

## Change control

Any change to the criteria, weights, applicability functions or thresholds requires:

1. A documented reason.
2. Updated unit tests.
3. A new rubric version displayed in the application.
4. Review of saved-result comparability.
